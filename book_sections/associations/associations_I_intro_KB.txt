## Chapter: Summarizing Associations I — Setup & Introduction

### Chapter Context

This chapter introduces how to **describe associations between variables**.
An association occurs when the value of one variable tends to differ depending on the value of another variable.

In biological research, associations appear when:

* traits differ across species or treatments
* probabilities differ across groups
* values of one measurement tend to increase or decrease with another

This chapter focuses on **describing associations**, not explaining their causes.

Students should learn to:

* recognize patterns between variables
* summarize those patterns using simple statistics
* visualize associations using plots
* interpret results carefully without assuming causation

The next chapter will extend these ideas to **associations between two numeric variables**, introducing covariance, correlation, and slope.

---

# Learning Goals

After completing this chapter students should be able to:

1. Recognize different types of relationships between variables.
2. Describe associations between a categorical explanatory variable and another variable.
3. Use conditional summaries (means or proportions) to describe group differences.
4. Visualize associations using plots such as bar plots, boxplots, and scatterplots.
5. Interpret associations cautiously and understand that **association does not imply causation**.
6. Connect statistical summaries to real biological questions.

---

# Types of Associations Introduced

This chapter focuses on two types of relationships.

### 1. Categorical–Categorical

Both variables represent categories.

Example biological questions:

* Are pink flowers more likely to receive pollinator visits?
* Do survival rates differ across habitat types?

Associations are described using:

* **conditional proportions**
* contingency tables
* bar plots

---

### 2. Categorical–Numeric

A categorical explanatory variable is used to compare a numeric outcome.

Example biological questions:

* Do plants receiving pollinator visits produce more seeds?
* Does trait value differ between two species?

Associations are described using:

* **conditional means**
* **difference in means**
* **Cohen's D** (a standardized difference)

---

# Key Concept: Conditional Summaries

Many association summaries in this chapter are **conditional summaries**.

A conditional summary describes a variable **within a specific group**.

Examples:

* probability of survival **given** passenger class
* average trait value **given** species
* proportion visited **given** flower color

Conditional summaries allow students to compare how outcomes differ across groups.

---

# Key Concept: Association vs Causation

An association means two variables vary together.

However:

* associations do **not automatically imply causation**
* patterns can arise from confounding variables
* patterns observed in one dataset may not generalize

Students should treat association summaries as **descriptions of patterns**, not proof of causal relationships.

---

# Common Student Confusions

Students often confuse:

**Raw counts vs proportions**

Example:
A bar plot with many observations may appear larger even if the **proportion** is the same.

---

**Overall mean vs conditional mean**

Students may mistakenly interpret group differences without recognizing that the **overall mean can differ from group means**.

---

**Association vs prediction**

A relationship observed in one dataset does not guarantee accurate prediction in another context.

# Tutor Guidance

When helping students:

* Encourage them to **describe patterns before computing statistics**
* Ask students **what variables are explanatory vs response**
* Prompt them to interpret **plots first**, then statistics
* Keep explanations short and ask guiding questions

The goal is to help students **recognize and describe associations**, not to perform formal statistical inference.
